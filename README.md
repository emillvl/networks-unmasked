# Networks Unmasked

How Data Travels, What It Reveals, and the Cost of Privacy

**Emil Valiyev · Definitive Edition · Version 1.0 · 26 October 2026**

[Download the complete PDF](https://github.com/emillvl/networks-unmasked/releases/download/v1.0.0/Networks-Unmasked-Definitive-Edition.pdf) · [Figshare](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.34792464.v1) · [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23671687) · [Leanpub](https://leanpub.com/networks-unmasked)

Networks Unmasked explains how a request moves through a computer and across a network, and what each stage reveals to an observer. Its 22 chapters cover host networking, addressing, routing, DNS, TCP and UDP, application protocols, TLS, firewalls, proxies, VPNs, Tor, and I2P.

Engineering case studies from [Ghostnector](https://github.com/emillvl/Ghostnector), the author's Linux network-privacy project, examine traffic confinement, lifecycle failures, verification, and performance measurement. The book distinguishes directly inspected evidence from reported test results and includes a separate reconstruction of recorded latency samples. The book and software are separate works.

The evidence boundary is Ghostnector commit `17a03c4` and technical sources consulted on 24 October 2026. The book has 108 pages, seven original explanatory figures, two appendices, and 68 reference entries. Its cases describe particular implementations and experiments; they do not establish universal privacy guarantees.
